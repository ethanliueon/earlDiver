# Art direction: the Margaret's locker

The UI is part of the boat: painted olive steel, as the locker door is. This page is the rule every screen is
checked against, and the audit that led to it.

## Why it read as generated (audit, 4.23.0)

1. **Six typefaces**, and a code font used for sentences.
2. **Everything captioned**: a caption under every setting, a label tape on every section, hints under every
   panel, a tagline and a three-part motto on the title.
3. **Middle-dot strings** in the chrome ("salvage · live specimens · deep survey").
4. **One glossy brass pill repeated** for every title, toggle, close coin and key.
5. **Material soup**: blueprint, brass port, typed paper, clipboards, green enamel tags, a brass plaque, drum
   counters, all on one screen.
6. **Rivets as a border pattern** round every panel.
7. **Siblings that disagree**: three different close buttons, three ways of showing cash, the aquarium in its
   own flat style.
8. **Unicode as icons** (lock, stars, ticks).

## The rules

- **One material.** Painted olive steel (tools/olive_kit.py): panels, wells, buttons. Brass, paper and blueprint
  are gone from the UI (paper stays only where the thing is paper: the journal, the catalog's screen).
- **One "press me".** A raised plate of cream deck paint (the Margaret's own white) with dark words. Secondary:
  a raised olive plate. Chosen: ringed in the same cream paint.
- **Two faces.** EB Garamond for every word; IBM Plex Mono only for figures (cash, depth, prices, counts).
- **Names are painted on**: a panel's name in cream paint on the steel, not on a plate stuck to it.
- **Captions earn their place**: only where a player would otherwise not know what something does.
- **No separators as decoration**: commas and line breaks, not " · ".
- **Icons are drawn**, never characters.
- **Same thing, same look**: one close button, one way to show cash, one row, one tab.
