# Polish plan 2: the game, not just the HUD

Played on a phone held sideways, by touch: the whole tutorial (deck, jump, swim, observe, the forced miss, the
catch, surfacing, claiming, the journal tour, selling, climbing aboard, the cabin), then drowning at 35 m, the thing
in the deep at 700 m, and a reef catch up close. About 90 screenshots, read frame by frame. Panels (inventory, shop,
catalogs, tank rooms) are left as they are, as asked.

## The vision

A diver alone over a deep that is mostly black. Everything he carries is old salt-eaten gear; everything that glows is
either his own weak lamp or something alive down there (cold blue-green; the thing's red). Help from the game should
feel like it comes from the sea and his gear, not from an app laid over it: no white arrows, no yellow boxes, no
dimmed screens with spotlights, no notification badges.

## What looks generated or generic, and what it becomes

### The tutorial's pointing (the most "app" thing in the game)
| Now | Seen in | Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| A trail of white `<` chevrons floating in the water | every swim step | A faint thread of glowing plankton drifting toward the goal, denser and brighter near it, gone once there |
| A hard white circle with a ripple round the fish | swim, observe, net steps | The fish itself catches a cold glow; four thin focus ticks (as a mask's sight or the camera's) close in on it as you near |
| A white paper-plane arrow and "22 m" at the screen edge | off-screen fish, surfacing | A soft cold glow bleeding in from that edge of the mask, the distance in small bone figures |
| Screen dimmed, the target cut out bright, a white `▼` above it | quest claim, Sell all, journal | No dimming; the target gets the same breathing cold ring as the HUD buttons, the rest of the panel stays readable |
| A yellow box round the cabin door, a yellow `←`; a white `↓` over the computer; `> > > >` across the cabin floor | deck, cabin | The thing itself lit in the scene: the door's porthole glows, the computer's screen brightens and flickers, the lamp over it swings |
| The tutorial's own olive button floating over the sea ("Sell your catch") | selling | The hand button by the net, ringed (as observe and the net already are) |
| The same instruction twice: the goal at the top left, a second line at the bottom | most steps | The top left says the goal; the bottom says only which button, and only when a button does it |

### The tutorial's pacing
- It rings the sprint button through **every** swim step ("Tap to sprint (uses air faster)") and the forced first miss
  sends the fish off: with the starting 30 s tank I ran out of air **inside the tutorial**, twice ("Out of air:
  surface!" while still on the first fish)
- Proposal: sprint is taught once, on the way back up (where it helps); the first fish swims slowly and close; the air
  cannot run out before the first catch is made

### Rewards
| Now | Becomes |
|---|---|
| "Quest done: +$50.00" in large plain white at the top | A paper tag slides in by the clipboard with the sum written on it, and settles into it |
| A card in the middle of the screen during play ("Flick net +1 NEW") | The item's picture drawn small and dropped into the bag button (which gives a tug) |
| A red "1" badge on the quest clipboard (a phone notification badge) | A small cold light on the clipboard's clip, slowly breathing |
| The catch's name at the top of the screen | Beside the fish, as observing already does |

### Moments that should hit harder
- **The net**: the swing leaves no trace; a miss and a catch look nearly the same until a word appears at the top.
  Becomes: a faint arc of disturbed water following the net, a puff of bubbles; a catch pulls the fish in with a flick
  and a tightening of the mesh; a miss shows the fish bolting with a streak
- **Drowning**: two text lines and he drifts. Becomes: as the air goes, the edges of vision close in and darken, a
  heartbeat in the vignette, the colour drains, his bubbles stop, then black
- **Sprinting**: only the button glows. Becomes: a wake of bubbles and a harder kick in his fins
- **The thing**: its art is strong (red-lit skull, a long line of teeth). The HUD's red cartoon eye beside the depth
  is not: it goes. Its presence becomes the gauge's glass misting red and its blip on the sonar
- **Warnings**: "Air critical", "Out of air", "Crush depth" are plain words on a smoky band. Becomes: danger lines
  shake and flicker; the air warning lives on the air gauge itself

### The HUD (as planned in round 1, kept)
- Right: one large centre button (the tool in hand, a swap chip on its rim to change it) and four fixed skill
  buttons round it: observe, hand, sprint, call sub; in the sub: leave, tank room, autopilot, trawl
- Pause to the top-right corner; the left column is the bag, the lamp and what is carried
- Symbols redrawn as one engraved line set made for this game (lamp with beam, glow stick, lure, crab pot, camera, net)
- The depth an instrument (a gauge with rolling digits); O2 and hull as its tubes; the quest clipboard, journal and
  chart as dark bronze plates instead of bright illustrations
- One type system (two Barlow styles and one small-caps label; Caveat only for handwriting); nothing under 11 pt

### The title (full cinematic)
Dark water, the logo stencilled on a plate, the camera drifting slowly down past the boat into the dark, a shape
passing under it, the thing's red eye opening if left idle; the divers as dive-log cards.

## Order of work
1. The tutorial's pointing and pacing (the biggest "made by AI" signal, and a real problem: drowning in the tutorial)
2. The HUD: layout, symbols, gauges, plates, type
3. The moments: net, drowning, sprint, the thing, warnings, rewards
4. The title cinematic
5. The same play-through and screenshot set again, before and after side by side
