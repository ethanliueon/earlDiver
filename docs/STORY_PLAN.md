# The main quest line: a proposal (6.7, not built)

## The idea in one line
A drowned researcher's field notebook, found page by page, leads the diver deeper; each page asks for a thing
(an animal, a photograph, an object at a marked place), never "go to X". The last pages are about the thing in the deep.

## Why a notebook
- It fits the game's voice: the world speaks (quest notes, Dave), Earl never does.
- It is collecting, which is what the game already is: pages are found, the journal fills, the chart gets marks.
- It gives every quest a reason and a place without a quest-giver standing on a dock.

## Characters
- **Dr. Ines Vahl** (never seen alive): a marine biologist who worked from the research station twenty years ago and
  went missing below 1,000 m. Her pages are dated, precise, a little more frightened each time.
- **Dave**: knew her. He sells what she left at the station, and his lines change as pages turn up ("She wrote that?").
- **The Old One**: takes old coins; knows more than it says. One late page is a drawing of the Old One's cave.
- **The thing in the deep**: Vahl called it "the Listener". The pages teach its rules (light, sound, the lanterns).

## How a page works (the shape of every quest)
1. A page is found: in a chest, in a wreck's locker, in a trap, inside a fish's stomach (rare), at the station.
2. Reading it puts a **circle on the chart** (a soft pencil ring, not a pin) and a line in the Log, in her words:
   "The pale shrimp swarm where the vent field meets the cliff. Bring me one alive."
3. The ask is one of: **catch** a named animal (alive, or a size), **photograph** one (a grade), **retrieve** an object
   lying in the circle (her camera, a sample jar, a lantern's key), **listen** (stay at a depth in the dark for 30 s
   with the lamp off), **light** (set a glow stick at a place).
4. Doing it gives the next page's whereabouts, cash, sometimes a piece of her gear (rare, unique, its own card).

## Chapters (depth ladder)
1. **The station's shelf (0-60 m)**: four pages. Her first notes, the reef animals, a camera with a roll in it.
2. **The kelp and the wrecks (60-200 m)**: five pages. A wreck's locker; a guardian jellyfish she named.
3. **Twilight (200-600 m)**: five pages. The first mention of the Listener; a recording to play back at a lantern.
4. **Midnight (600-1,500 m)**: four pages. The vent field, the brine pool, her sub's hull lying on the floor.
5. **The last pages (1,500 m+)**: three pages, the handwriting failing. The final ask is to bring her notebook's last
   page back up. What happens then is the ending (left open for the owner).

## Rules to keep it from feeling like a chore
- Never more than one page active; the next is always one circle on the chart.
- Every ask uses a system the player already has (net, camera, trap, trawl, glow stick, chamber).
- Circles are generous (40-80 m) and the thing is found inside them by looking, not by a marker on it.
- Pages are short, in her voice, never instructions ("The light keeps it off. Mostly.").
- The board quests stay as side work for cash.

## Questions for the owner
- Is a dead researcher the right tone, or should she be alive somewhere (a voice on the radio)?
- Should the ending be a choice (give the page to Dave / to the Old One / keep it)?
- Can the thing be named in the game ("the Listener"), or must it stay unnamed?
